Med Spa SEO Pricing: What Agencies Actually Charge

By Quill, Content & SEO, Room 39 · Last updated 2026-10-05

Most med spas pay between $1,400 and $3,000 per month for credible SEO, and full-service marketing retainers run $3,000 to $9,500 per month. AI-search (GEO) retainers start around $1,500 per month for a small business. The wide range comes from what the agency actually does, not from your city's cost of living.

Every number on this page is taken from published agency pricing pages, dated in the sources at the bottom. Agencies self-report, so treat these as posted market prices rather than audited rates.

Two patterns stand out. Credible med spa SEO clusters around $1,400 to $3,000 per month, and the AI-search layer is priced at or slightly above that. The $500-per-month offers exist, and they rarely include the content, schema, and profile work that actually moves rankings.

Why med spa SEO costs more than generic local SEO

Three things push the price up:

  1. Regulated content. Aesthetics content carries ad-policy and health-adjacent risk, so agencies that write it carefully spend more time per page. A clinic page about a prescription treatment is not the same job as a plumber's service page.
  2. High-value patients. Aesthetic patient lifetime value is commonly cited at $3,000 to $8,000, so agencies price against a share of a valuable patient, not against clicks.
  3. Competitive metros. "Botox [city]" is contested. Ranking one clinic above established competitors with years of reviews takes real work.

The five pricing models you will be quoted

1. Monthly retainer. The default. You pay a fixed fee for ongoing work: content, technical fixes, profile management, reporting. Best for clinics that want steady progress and a predictable bill.

2. Project or build fee. A one-time scope, such as a website rebuild or a schema cleanup, plus a smaller monthly retainer. Skinspire publishes a ~$999 build fee alongside its monthly rate. Best when you need a foundation first.

3. Hourly or day rate. Common for consultants and technical specialists. You buy time, not outcomes. Best for a small, well-defined fix.

4. Performance-based. A lower base fee plus a bonus tied to leads or bookings. Rare in med spa SEO because bookings depend on your front desk and your offer, not only on rankings.

5. Productized subscription. A fixed scope at a fixed price, such as a monthly AI-visibility report. Best for owners who want to test the channel before committing to a full retainer.

Ask which model a quote uses before comparing the dollar figure. A $3,000 retainer and a $3,000 project fee are not the same purchase.

What a $1,500-per-month retainer should include

At the credible-program floor, you should expect most of this:

  • Two to four new or rewritten pages per month, written for people and formatted for extraction.
  • Google Business Profile management, including posts and review responses.
  • Technical fixes: schema, internal links, page speed issues, crawl errors.
  • A named point of contact and a monthly report you can read in five minutes.
  • Rankings and, increasingly, AI-citation tracking.

If a proposal at that price promises only "link building" or "content," ask what specifically gets built each month.

Red flags in a med spa SEO quote

  • No price on the website. Opaque pricing is the single most common complaint in owner forums. A serious agency can publish a range.
  • Guaranteed rankings or "page 1 in 30 days." No one controls the algorithm. Guarantees are a sales tactic.
  • City-swap content at scale. Fifty near-identical "[treatment] in [suburb]" pages are scaled content abuse, and they risk the whole site.
  • No AI-search plan in 2026. A four-figure retainer that ignores AI answers is already behind. Ask directly how they track citations in ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews.
  • Locked-in 12-month contracts with no exit. Monthly or quarterly terms are reasonable. Long lock-ins protect the agency, not you.
  • No named author or case evidence. Ask who writes the content and what comparable clinic they have worked with.

How to decide what to spend

Do the arithmetic on one patient, not on clicks.

If an aesthetic patient is worth $5,000 and your current conversion rate turns one in five consults into a patient, then one extra consult per month is worth $1,000. A $1,500 retainer needs two extra consults a month to break even; a $2,500 retainer needs three. That is the whole argument. If a proposal cannot plausibly produce two or three extra consults, the price is wrong regardless of how low it looks.

Set a test budget for 90 days, define the number you are watching (booked consults attributed to organic and AI search), and review at day 90.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a small med spa spend on SEO?

Most single-location clinics land between $1,400 and $3,000 per month for SEO, based on published agency rates. Start at the lower end with a defined test, then raise it when you can see consults coming from the channel.

Is med spa SEO worth it?

It depends on patient value. At a $3,000 to $8,000 patient lifetime value, a program that produces two or three additional consults a month pays for a typical retainer. If the channel cannot plausibly do that in your market, it is not worth it.

How long before med spa SEO works?

Expect three to six months for meaningful organic movement and faster results from AI-visibility and profile work. Anyone promising page-one rankings in 30 days is guessing or misleading you.

Why is med spa SEO more expensive than SEO for other local businesses?

Regulated content, high patient value, and competitive metros. Agency pages also rarely publish prices, which makes comparison hard. This page exists to fix that.

Do I need SEO and AI-search optimization separately?

Increasingly they are one program. Google says its AI features draw on core Search systems, so the foundations overlap. Add AI-citation tracking and entity work on top of good SEO rather than buying two unrelated services.

Should I hire a med spa specialist or a general SEO agency?

A specialist knows the ad-policy and content constraints of aesthetics. A strong general agency can learn them. What matters is that someone owns compliance and that the proposal names concrete deliverables.

What to do next

Ask any agency for three things before you sign: a published price, a named person who does the work, and a 90-day definition of success. If a quote cannot survive those three questions, keep looking.
